# Notes for future work

- This is a simple website with no build step.
- The main files are `index.html`, `main.css`, `app.js` and `sw.js`.
- `strokes.js` holds the stroke-order data for writing the name. `app.js` loads it after the page has loaded.
- `mountains.js` draws realistic 3D mountains with the Fuji-like peak (WebGL 2). The app uses it for the autumn mountains (settings in `MTN3D` in `app.js`); `bamboo.html` is the test bed for tuning them. In the app they are drawn in two passes timed to about 8 ms of the graphics chip a frame (`MTN_BUDGET`; `Mountains.start(o, 8, mode, budget)`): the mesh once (only each pixel's distance is kept), then the light and shadows band by band, then read back a strip at a time, so they never make a frame jerk. They start while the koi dives and are drawn while the name is written; the rest of the scenery that waits for them (`landFinishScenery`) is held until the name stands still. The picture is the same as drawing it in 8 even bands (to within rounding). The graphics contexts and shaders of the mountains, the trunks, the lake water and the ink fluid are made on the name screen (`dynWarmGpu` in `app.js`), so none of them pauses the scene the first time it is drawn.
- `trunks.js` draws the autumn maples' trunk and branches in 3D (WebGL 2, every frame, so they sway): round limbs that grow out of each other at the forks, Japanese-maple bark, root flare, moss, and light that matches the leaves and the time of day and weather (with 木漏れ日 spots under the canopy). The app uses it for the autumn trees (settings in `TRUNK` in `app.js`; `buildKoyoTrunk`, `koyoTrunkLight` and `fillKoyoBranches` there); `bamboo.html` is the test bed for tuning them (its panel's 木 Tree page; "Copy values" gives the numbers to move into `TRUNK`). If the tree ever loses its graphics context on the tablet, `trunks.js` remembers it for 12 hours and draws the trees flat (a safety latch, so it can't keep taking the graphics chip from the mountains). `tree-check.html` is the check page for the tablet: it shows what the graphics chip reports, whether the latch is on (with a button to switch the trees back on), and runs the tree drawing for a minute with live counters.
- `lakeside.js` holds the painters for the autumn lake scene: cedar (sugi), pine, ginkgo and maple trees, a 3D five-storey pagoda and shrine hall, mossy rocks, red spider lilies and swaying susuki. The cedars are drawn as stacked tiers of foliage like a painted fir: flat bands of colour (no gradients), lumpy edges made of uneven tufts, each tree different from its seed; their look is set with `Lakeside.sugiLook` from the `cd…` values and the cedar colours. The app's autumn scene is the lake scene (section 8c of `app.js`, "THE LAKE", values in `LAKE`; it also sets the eye line, `LAND.horizon`, the torii line, `TORII.path`, and the lanterns' and gates' depth of field in `LANT` and `TORII`). It is baked a part a frame with the rest of the landscape (`updateLandscape`, steps 5, 6, 8, 2, 7, 3, 4). `bamboo.html` is the test bed for tuning it (section 7c there, values in `LK`, its panel's 湖 Lake page; "Copy values" gives the numbers to move into `LAKE`). The scene: a small lake, a near wooded headland with the pagoda on the left, the torii line coming toward you from far away, a torii in the water, trees on the right bank, and the near bank; the middle is kept clear for the text. In autumn the swipe hint and the page dots stand on the lake (`HINT_NAV_Y` in `app.js`, the panel's "Swipe button and dots: how far down"), with the app's own hint text.
- In `bamboo.html` the trees along the right bank of the lake are the smaller (far) main tree, many times over (`bakeBank`, `drawBank`, `updateBank` in section 7c; `buildTree(true, P)` makes each one). Each has its own seed, leaf colour, fullness and lean, the same 3D trunk, the same sway (`swayTree`), the same gust push and the same falling leaves, all scaled by its on-screen size (`T.size`). Their depth of field is `bankFocus`, `bankBlur…`, `bankHaze…` in `LK` (blur is done by drawing a blurrier tree on a coarser layer, which is also cheaper). The settings panel has a "Jump to section" drop-down at the top. The row runs from the screen's edge back to the torii set by `bankLastGate` (default 2: the second gate from the front); no tree stands further back than that, so fewer trees are drawn. The app has them too (`BankTrees` in section 8c of `app.js`, inside `DynBG`; settings `bankX0` … `bankHazeFar` in `LAKE`). There, to keep it smooth: each tree is drawn into a picture of its own and laid down each frame leaning with its sway (a shear about its foot); it is drawn afresh only when its limbs have moved more than about half a pixel past what the lean shows, or the light changed, a few a frame (about 2 ms, those that moved most first); its 3D trunk has a picture of its own too (with the shade on the ground and the fallen leaves at its foot), laid down leaning with the trunk's own sway and drawn again only when a limb has moved more than that lean shows, or the light has really changed (`bigLightV`); trees off the screen or wholly behind the right-hand main tree's leaves are not drawn (that tree's leaves are fully opaque: `koyoLeafAlpha`; the left-hand one keeps `KOYO.nearOpacity` / `farOpacity`); one shared set of clump pictures serves every tree; the row is planted again only when the screen or the settings change; each tree's first picture is made while the name stands still. `DynBG.bankInfo()` gives counts and timings in the console.
- `water.js` draws the lake's surface on the graphics chip (WebGL 2) for `bamboo.html`: the mirrored scene rippled pixel by pixel by a normal map (`textures/waternormals.jpg`, from three.js, MIT licence), the deep water colour near you (Fresnel) and the sun's sparkle, the method of three.js's Water. It has the same 12-hour safety latch as `trunks.js` (the page then draws its own 2D water); the Lake panel shows which is in use and has a button to switch it back on. The app uses it for the lake too (`lakeDrawWater` in `app.js`; it is in `ASSETS` in `sw.js`). `Water.init(url, { recover: true, latchKey })` (asked for only by `newwinter.html`; the app and the other test beds call `Water.init(url)` and behave exactly as before): when Chrome takes the context away (it keeps 16 WebGL contexts across the open tabs and drops the oldest, and never gives such a one back) a fresh context is made and the normal map and mirror loaded into it again; the 12-hour switch-off only after 3 losses in 10 minutes, under the page's own key (`waterLatchNewWinter`), never the app's `waterLatch1`. The panel on `bamboo.html` can be dragged by its title bar.
- `koi.js` draws the koi on the start button from code (no image): the koi chosen in `KOI_LOOK.kinds` (up to 4, each a different variety: kohaku, ogon, showa, tancho, sanke, platinum) swimming the pond inside the ring like real fish, in moods picked at random (cruise, slow, rest, dart, sudden turn). Each body follows its head like a rope with a soft bend limit (it never folds or twitches). Each koi dives to a different depth each time and swims about down there; one at a time comes up for air; two about to meet at the same depth: the deeper dips under; no koi rises or dives through another (it swims to a clear spot first). Touched, a koi darts off; on swipe up all swim forward and down (`koiPond.leave()` in `triggerKoiStart`; the start screen waits `KOI_AWAY_MS` so they are gone before it fades). Settings in `KOI_LOOK` in `app.js`, including the ring size (`ring`, applied as `--koi-ring` in `main.css`: the ring, glow and pond grow, the koi keep their size). `koi2.html` is the test bed ("Copy values" gives the numbers to move into `KOI_LOOK`; it keeps its own saved values); `koi.html` is the earlier one. It draws only while the start screen is showing.
- `fireworks.js` draws a night fireworks show after the Nagaoka festival. The app uses it in the autumn scene at night (settings in `FIREWORKS` in `app.js`); `fireworks.html` is the test bed for tuning it.
- `clouds.js` draws the realistic day clouds (cumulus) in the sky. The app uses it for the clouds around the sun (settings in `CLOUD_LOOK` in `app.js`); `clouds.html` is the quick test bed for tuning them, and `bamboo-clouds.html` (a copy of `bamboo.html`) tunes them in the full scene, with values for each time of day and weather (`CLOUD` in its script).
- `winter2.html` (a copy of `bamboo.html`) is the test bed for the new winter scene. It is drawn with `mountains.js` (its winter mode: `winter`, `forestSnow`, `snowSlope`), `snowfield.js` (the lit snowfield), `torii.js` (the 3D torii with snow), `conifers.js` (snowy firs and frosted trees), `snowlights.js` (snow lanterns, snow huts, the stone lantern), `snowheaps.js` (the snow on the plum branches; with the snow material in `snowfield.js`, the lit snow on the branches and the torii) and `clouds.js`. `snow-lab.html` is the quick test bed for that snow, seen large. `winter2.html` now shows the new winter layout from the user's reference picture (`V.ref`, on by default; the panel's "The reference scene" group; off shows the older layout), with every element where it stands in that picture (the user's rule: do not change the layout): a misty hill of firs with a five-storey pagoda (left), a shrine hall, a row of torii with lit stone lanterns along a stepping-stone path, frosted trees, a steaming pond that mirrors the scene live (each column about the far edge of the water), two cranes on its bank, roped pines (雪吊り) on the right and Fuji with its summit at `fujiX` 0.81, `fujiHeight` 0.365. Everything is drawn in code (no pictures, as everywhere in the project; the user's element sheet is only the reference for how each thing looks): the pagoda, hall, stone lanterns, cranes and roped pine by `winterpieces.js` (each laid out in the sheet's own measurements), the torii by `torii.js`, and the hill of firs, the pond's banks, the stepping-stone path and the other roped firs by `winterscene.js`. The torii, the stone lanterns and the stepping-stone path are one structure on the ground, as in the reference (`REF.approach`, `approachLayout()`; the user's rules): the path meanders as the user drew it on the reference (an S, `approach.line`, made smooth on the ground): up-left from the bottom edge, its left bend widened so it passes through the front torii (the user's choice), up-right to a bend beyond the big lantern, back left, and on to the shrine's steps; 6 torii (the user's choice, `approach.gates`) at equal distances along it (`approach.every`), all facing the viewer, with a clear gap left before the shrine; one stone lantern on each side in every gap between two gates, each side at its own place in the gap as the autumn scene places them (`torPlaceLanterns` in `app.js`: `rightAlong`, `leftAlong`), standing straight to the left or right of the stones on the screen and moved out only until it clears every stone (`fromStones`). Everything is in perspective about the ground's own eye line (`approach.eye`, 0.62, worked out from the torii's sizes in the reference; not `V.horizon`, the line where the snowfield meets the mist): a thing at ground depth Z shows 1/Z as large. The path's shape is set by the user: nine points it curves through (`V.path1x` … `V.path9y`, `pathPoints()`; the panel's "Stone path" group, or the numbered handles on the picture, dragged; the torii and lanterns follow it), with `pathWidth`, `stoneSize`, `stoneDepth`, `stoneGap` (these replace `REF.approach.line` and `REF.stone…`). The stones are laid as in the user's close-up of the reference (`WinterScene.path`): level rows of flat slabs filling a band of even width centred on the path's smooth line (both edges smooth, an S), one row behind another with the same gap between every two (in perspective), each row one slab or two/three pieces with the same gap; a piece that would come closer than the gap to another or to a measured slab is trimmed or shortened, or left out (never touching or overlapping: two touching read as one broken piece, which the user rejected); any hole left inside the band between stones is filled with a fitted stone; each stone then grows a little toward a slightly farther neighbour so gaps are even. The layout is kept (`layoutCache`) as the snowfield's slices all draw it. Each stone's top is rendered pixel by pixel (`stoneRaster`, cached): clean flat slate with gentle tone and faint brush streaks (no snow lumps, frost speckle, pebbles, leaves or moss: the reference has none), a crisp dark line along its front, a thin lip of snow along its back, a side face of varying thickness half buried by banked snow, a thin crease and a short shadow on the snow away from the sun (no halo). The stones are drawn sharp over each snowfield slice after the slice is softened (`drawStones` in `winter2.html`), so none is blurred. The four nearest stones (the two nearest slabs and the two just beyond them) are set exactly where the reference has them (`REF.nearSlabs`: their four corners on the screen, measured on the reference) and drawn crisp on the banks' layer (`WinterScene.slabs`, through the same painter as every stone, `slabPoly`; not in the snowfield's softened layer): dark slate matched to the reference's colour and range, a thin dark inked edge toward the viewer, a mottled top, a soft blue shadow; the stones along the path start just beyond them: the first is moved across so its right end is a small gap (as between the reference's stones) from the leftmost of them, so no gap opens there. These torii use `torii.js`'s `darkTop` option (the whole top beam black, as in the sheet; off by default, so the app's torii are unchanged). In this scene the snowfield is one smooth piece (its second swell lies below the screen, and the distance is hazed only by the ground mist), so no strips show. As in the app, the lamps and windows are lit only in the evening and at night (`nightLights()`), and the pond's colour and reflection follow the light. Where everything stands is `REF` in section 8b of the page's script, measured on the reference picture (fractions of the screen). - `reveal-lab.html` (a copy of `bamboo.html`) is the test bed for bringing the autumn scene in with wet ink washes, one part after another (Fuji, the ridges, the lake, the headland with the pagoda and shrine, the torii line and lanterns, the bank trees, the maples, the bamboo, the near shore; the sun and clouds, the lanterns' light, the birds, the falling leaves and the text fade in). The washes are drawn by `inkreveal.js` with `inkfluid.js`: wet on wet, the fluid of Inkwash by Jonathan Whitaker (MIT licence, credited in the files) ported to its own small WebGL 2 canvas (the water swirls and carries the ink, which feathers into wet paper and stops at the dry edge; each part's ink is one channel, so neighbouring parts bleed into each other). Each part's drops become brush strokes: clear water first, then the ink into it, a little different every play. If WebGL 2 isn't available it falls back to a simple wet paper on the main processor (the panel's "Wet paper" choice). Section 10b of the page's script holds the parts and where their drops fall; its panel holds only the reveal's values (the scene uses `bamboo.html`'s saved values as they are). Its first panel holds the app's first message (the greeting: 「桜庭さん、初めまして。」, the seasonal line, the weather line and the closing). Its last part, "The first message panel", times that message on its own (its paragraphs come in one after another, rising a little); in the app its start is `RV_MESSAGE_AT`. Its main maples are shaped as in the app (`shapeCanopy`, `fillTrunkSide`: dome-shaped underside, no holes, the canopy carried past the screen edge) and the right-hand one's leaves are opaque; when the app's scene changes, merge `bamboo.html`'s changes into it and port any app-only ones. The app uses it too: in autumn, when the scene first comes in after the name, it is painted in by the reveal instead of fading in (section 8e of `app.js`, "THE INK REVEAL": values in `RV_LOOK` and `RV_PARTS`, where each part's drops fall in `rvSeeds`; move values from the panel's "Copy values" into them). There the lanterns' light comes with the torii, and the text, button and toolbar keep the app's own timing. The name stands still until everything the scene needs has been made (`dynSceneReady`: the landscape baked, the trees' clumps and first leaves, each part's drops in `rvPrecompute`; at most `NAME_WAIT_MAX`, 6 s, longer), so no heavy work is left for the reveal's frames. The reveal starts on the first frame after the name begins to fade (no pause): the sun and sky come in at once and every other part waits only until what it shows has been baked (`rvReady`, `InkReveal.release`), then starts at its own time or straight away; the first message comes at `RV_MESSAGE_AT` (4.0 s) on the reveal's clock (`rvBambooIn`). The fluid's graphics context is set up while the name stands still and its pictures are let go when the reveal ends; if the graphics chip can't be used it falls back to the simple wet paper (a safety stop after 40 s shows whatever is left). `inkreveal.js` copies the fluid's tiles once a frame into an ordinary canvas and cuts every part's mask from that copy (drawing a WebGL canvas straight into a 2D one makes the browser copy it each time). `window.RVDebug.log` has its timeline for the console.
- While the scene runs, nothing big is re-made for small changes of the light: the leaves and the trees' clumps follow every change (`lookVersion`, a few a frame), but the landscape and the bamboo are re-made only when the light has really changed (`bigLightV`: a new part of the day, or a change past `BIG_LIGHT_STEP`). The clouds are painted in the background by `clouds.js`'s worker (`Clouds.paintAsync`). The leaves a gust shakes off come one a frame (`gustDrops`).

### Gotchas already hit (do not repeat)

- Flat 3D faces make snow look like polygons: paint snow as soft bands (`softBands`) with a blurred copy underneath; never blur thin bands themselves (they turn see-through). Fading by see-through bands leaves stripes: paint solid on a layer and fade it in ONE radial or linear sweep (`destination-out`).
- Blur filters on a full-screen canvas at high scale hang the page: draw each element on a layer just its own size (the pagoda does; each ROW of firs is on one layer and blurred ONCE; blurring each tree separately took 25 s).
- When a hand-built 3D face's corners are twisted (upturned eaves), take its normal from all edges (Newell), not three corners.
- Edit order matters in `winterhill.js`'s `pagoda`: the drawing loop must come after every helper and table it uses.
- The thin outline every face gets (to hide hairline gaps) must use ROUND joins: mitred joins shoot out as spikes at sharp corners. Drawing back faces both ways round did not fix that and showed a wrong wedge.
