# Notes for future work

- This is a simple website with no build step.
- The main files are `index.html`, `main.css`, `app.js` and `sw.js`.
- `strokes.js` holds the stroke-order data for writing the name. `app.js` loads it after the page has loaded.
- `mountains.js` draws realistic 3D mountains with the Fuji-like peak (WebGL 2). The app uses it for the autumn mountains (settings in `MTN3D` in `app.js`); `bamboo.html` is the test bed for tuning them. In the app they are drawn in two passes timed to about 8 ms of the graphics chip a frame (`MTN_BUDGET`; `Mountains.start(o, 8, mode, budget)`): the mesh once (only each pixel's distance is kept), then the light and shadows band by band, then read back a strip at a time, so they never make a frame jerk. They start while the koi dives and are drawn while the name is written; the rest of the scenery that waits for them (`landFinishScenery`) is held until the name stands still. The picture is the same as drawing it in 8 even bands (to within rounding). The graphics contexts and shaders of the mountains, the trunks, the lake water and the ink fluid are made on the name screen (`dynWarmGpu` in `app.js`), so none of them pauses the scene the first time it is drawn.
- `trunks.js` draws the autumn maples' trunk and branches in 3D (WebGL 2, every frame, so they sway): round limbs that grow out of each other at the forks, Japanese-maple bark, root flare, moss, and light that matches the leaves and the time of day and weather (with 木漏れ日 spots under the canopy). The app uses it for the autumn trees (settings in `TRUNK` in `app.js`; `buildKoyoTrunk`, `koyoTrunkLight` and `fillKoyoBranches` there); `bamboo.html` is the test bed for tuning them (its panel's 木 Tree page; "Copy values" gives the numbers to move into `TRUNK`). If the tree ever loses its graphics context on the tablet, `trunks.js` remembers it for 12 hours and draws the trees flat (a safety latch, so it can't keep taking the graphics chip from the mountains). `tree-check.html` is the check page for the tablet: it shows what the graphics chip reports, whether the latch is on (with a button to switch the trees back on), and runs the tree drawing for a minute with live counters.
- `lakeside.js` holds the painters for the autumn lake scene: cedar (sugi), pine, ginkgo and maple trees, a 3D five-storey pagoda and shrine hall, mossy rocks, red spider lilies and swaying susuki. The cedars are drawn as stacked tiers of foliage like a painted fir: flat bands of colour (no gradients), lumpy edges made of uneven tufts, each tree different from its seed; their look is set with `Lakeside.sugiLook` from the `cd…` values and the cedar colours. The app's autumn scene is the lake scene (section 8c of `app.js`, "THE LAKE", values in `LAKE`; it also sets the eye line, `LAND.horizon`, the torii line, `TORII.path`, and the lanterns' and gates' depth of field in `LANT` and `TORII`). It is baked a part a frame with the rest of the landscape (`updateLandscape`, steps 5, 6, 8, 2, 7, 3, 4). `bamboo.html` is the test bed for tuning it (section 7c there, values in `LK`, its panel's 湖 Lake page; "Copy values" gives the numbers to move into `LAKE`). The scene: a small lake, a near wooded headland with the pagoda on the left, the torii line coming toward you from far away, a torii in the water, trees on the right bank, and the near bank; the middle is kept clear for the text. In autumn the swipe hint and the page dots stand on the lake (`HINT_NAV_Y` in `app.js`, the panel's "Swipe button and dots: how far down"), with the app's own hint text.
- In `bamboo.html` the trees along the right bank of the lake are the smaller (far) main tree, many times over (`bakeBank`, `drawBank`, `updateBank` in section 7c; `buildTree(true, P)` makes each one). Each has its own seed, leaf colour, fullness and lean, the same 3D trunk, the same sway (`swayTree`), the same gust push and the same falling leaves, all scaled by its on-screen size (`T.size`). Their depth of field is `bankFocus`, `bankBlur…`, `bankHaze…` in `LK` (blur is done by drawing a blurrier tree on a coarser layer, which is also cheaper). The settings panel has a "Jump to section" drop-down at the top. The row runs from the screen's edge back to the torii set by `bankLastGate` (default 2: the second gate from the front); no tree stands further back than that, so fewer trees are drawn. The app has them too (`BankTrees` in section 8c of `app.js`, inside `DynBG`; settings `bankX0` … `bankHazeFar` in `LAKE`). There, to keep it smooth: each tree is drawn into a picture of its own and laid down each frame leaning with its sway (a shear about its foot); it is drawn afresh only when its limbs have moved more than about half a pixel past what the lean shows, or the light changed, a few a frame (about 2 ms, those that moved most first); its 3D trunk has a picture of its own too (with the shade on the ground and the fallen leaves at its foot), laid down leaning with the trunk's own sway and drawn again only when a limb has moved more than that lean shows, or the light has really changed (`bigLightV`); trees off the screen or wholly behind the right-hand main tree's leaves are not drawn (that tree's leaves are fully opaque: `koyoLeafAlpha`; the left-hand one keeps `KOYO.nearOpacity` / `farOpacity`); one shared set of clump pictures serves every tree; the row is planted again only when the screen or the settings change; each tree's first picture is made while the name stands still. `DynBG.bankInfo()` gives counts and timings in the console.
- `water.js` draws the lake's surface on the graphics chip (WebGL 2) for `bamboo.html`: the mirrored scene rippled pixel by pixel by a normal map (`textures/waternormals.jpg`, from three.js, MIT licence), the deep water colour near you (Fresnel) and the sun's sparkle, the method of three.js's Water. It has the same 12-hour safety latch as `trunks.js` (the page then draws its own 2D water); the Lake panel shows which is in use and has a button to switch it back on. The app uses it for the lake too (`lakeDrawWater` in `app.js`; it is in `ASSETS` in `sw.js`). The panel on `bamboo.html` can be dragged by its title bar.
- `koi.js` draws the koi on the start button from code (no image): the koi chosen in `KOI_LOOK.kinds` (up to 4, each a different variety: kohaku, ogon, showa, tancho, sanke, platinum) swimming the pond inside the ring like real fish, in moods picked at random (cruise, slow, rest, dart, sudden turn). Each body follows its head like a rope with a soft bend limit (it never folds or twitches). Each koi dives to a different depth each time and swims about down there; one at a time comes up for air; two about to meet at the same depth: the deeper dips under; no koi rises or dives through another (it swims to a clear spot first). Touched, a koi darts off; on swipe up all swim forward and down (`koiPond.leave()` in `triggerKoiStart`; the start screen waits `KOI_AWAY_MS` so they are gone before it fades). Settings in `KOI_LOOK` in `app.js`, including the ring size (`ring`, applied as `--koi-ring` in `main.css`: the ring, glow and pond grow, the koi keep their size). `koi2.html` is the test bed ("Copy values" gives the numbers to move into `KOI_LOOK`; it keeps its own saved values); `koi.html` is the earlier one. It draws only while the start screen is showing.
- `fireworks.js` draws a night fireworks show after the Nagaoka festival. The app uses it in the autumn scene at night (settings in `FIREWORKS` in `app.js`); `fireworks.html` is the test bed for tuning it.
- `clouds.js` draws the realistic day clouds (cumulus) in the sky. The app uses it for the clouds around the sun (settings in `CLOUD_LOOK` in `app.js`); `clouds.html` is the quick test bed for tuning them, and `bamboo-clouds.html` (a copy of `bamboo.html`) tunes them in the full scene, with values for each time of day and weather (`CLOUD` in its script).
- `winter2.html` (a copy of `bamboo.html`) is the test bed for the new winter scene. It is drawn with `mountains.js` (its winter mode: `winter`, `forestSnow`, `snowSlope`), `snowfield.js` (the lit snowfield), `torii.js` (the 3D torii with snow), `conifers.js` (snowy firs and frosted trees), `snowlights.js` (snow lanterns, snow huts, the stone lantern), `snowheaps.js` (the snow on the plum branches; with the snow material in `snowfield.js`, the lit snow on the branches and the torii) and `clouds.js`. `snow-lab.html` is the quick test bed for that snow, seen large. `winter2.html` now shows the new winter layout from the user's reference picture (`V.ref`, on by default; the panel's "The reference scene" group; off shows the older layout), with every element where it stands in that picture (the user's rule: do not change the layout): a misty hill of firs with a five-storey pagoda (left), a shrine hall, a row of torii with lit stone lanterns along a stepping-stone path, frosted trees, a steaming pond that mirrors the scene live (each column about the far edge of the water), two cranes on its bank, roped pines (雪吊り) on the right and Fuji with its summit at `fujiX` 0.81, `fujiHeight` 0.365. Everything is drawn in code (no pictures, as everywhere in the project; the user's element sheet is only the reference for how each thing looks): the pagoda, hall, stone lanterns, cranes and roped pine by `winterpieces.js` (each laid out in the sheet's own measurements), the torii by `torii.js`, and the hill of firs, the pond's banks, the stepping-stone path and the other roped firs by `winterscene.js`. The torii, the stone lanterns and the stepping-stone path are one structure on the ground, as in the reference (`REF.approach`, `approachLayout()`; the user's rules): the path meanders as the user drew it on the reference (an S, `approach.line`, made smooth on the ground): up-left from the bottom edge, its left bend widened so it passes through the front torii (the user's choice), up-right to a bend beyond the big lantern, back left, and on to the shrine's steps; 6 torii (the user's choice, `approach.gates`) at equal distances along it (`approach.every`), all facing the viewer, with a clear gap left before the shrine; one stone lantern on each side in every gap between two gates, each side at its own place in the gap as the autumn scene places them (`torPlaceLanterns` in `app.js`: `rightAlong`, `leftAlong`), standing straight to the left or right of the stones on the screen and moved out only until it clears every stone (`fromStones`). Everything is in perspective about the ground's own eye line (`approach.eye`, 0.62, worked out from the torii's sizes in the reference; not `V.horizon`, the line where the snowfield meets the mist): a thing at ground depth Z shows 1/Z as large. The stones are laid as a mason would (`WinterScene.path`): each goes only as far on as it needs to clear the last one (a step back or a step aside, whichever is shorter along the path there), so in the near sweep they stack up the curve, each behind the last and overlapping it sideways, as in the reference; uneven gaps; a long slab here, two or three smaller pieces side by side there, a short one set off to one side; each its own shape (rounded, uneven corners), its long side level, turned a little. The four nearest stones (the two nearest slabs and the two just beyond them) are set exactly where the reference has them (`REF.nearSlabs`: their four corners on the screen, measured on the reference) and drawn crisp on the banks' layer (`WinterScene.slabs`, not in the snowfield's softened layer): dark slate matched to the reference's colour and range, a thin dark inked edge toward the viewer, a mottled top, a soft blue shadow; the stones along the path start just beyond them: the first is moved across so its right end is a small gap (as between the reference's stones) from the leftmost of them, so no gap opens there. These torii use `torii.js`'s `darkTop` option (the whole top beam black, as in the sheet; off by default, so the app's torii are unchanged). In this scene the snowfield is one smooth piece (its second swell lies below the screen, and the distance is hazed only by the ground mist), so no strips show. As in the app, the lamps and windows are lit only in the evening and at night (`nightLights()`), and the pond's colour and reflection follow the light. Where everything stands is `REF` in section 8b of the page's script, measured on the reference picture (fractions of the screen). The wind chime, the camellias and the nandina are left out (the user's choice). The page now keeps its values under a new key (`winterLab2-ref`), so the older saved values don't move the new layout. Its panel holds every value and can be dragged by its title bar. The app's winter theme uses these files (section 8d of `app.js`, settings in `WINT` and `WINT_M`; winter clouds in `CLOUD_WINTER`); move values from the panel's "Copy values" into them.
- `reveal-lab.html` (a copy of `bamboo.html`) is the test bed for bringing the autumn scene in with wet ink washes, one part after another (Fuji, the ridges, the lake, the headland with the pagoda and shrine, the torii line and lanterns, the bank trees, the maples, the bamboo, the near shore; the sun and clouds, the lanterns' light, the birds, the falling leaves and the text fade in). The washes are drawn by `inkreveal.js` with `inkfluid.js`: wet on wet, the fluid of Inkwash by Jonathan Whitaker (MIT licence, credited in the files) ported to its own small WebGL 2 canvas (the water swirls and carries the ink, which feathers into wet paper and stops at the dry edge; each part's ink is one channel, so neighbouring parts bleed into each other). Each part's drops become brush strokes: clear water first, then the ink into it, a little different every play. If WebGL 2 isn't available it falls back to a simple wet paper on the main processor (the panel's "Wet paper" choice). Section 10b of the page's script holds the parts and where their drops fall; its panel holds only the reveal's values (the scene uses `bamboo.html`'s saved values as they are). Its first panel holds the app's first message (the greeting: 「桜庭さん、初めまして。」, the seasonal line, the weather line and the closing). Its last part, "The first message panel", times that message on its own (its paragraphs come in one after another, rising a little); in the app its start is `RV_MESSAGE_AT`. Its main maples are shaped as in the app (`shapeCanopy`, `fillTrunkSide`: dome-shaped underside, no holes, the canopy carried past the screen edge) and the right-hand one's leaves are opaque; when the app's scene changes, merge `bamboo.html`'s changes into it and port any app-only ones. The app uses it too: in autumn, when the scene first comes in after the name, it is painted in by the reveal instead of fading in (section 8e of `app.js`, "THE INK REVEAL": values in `RV_LOOK` and `RV_PARTS`, where each part's drops fall in `rvSeeds`; move values from the panel's "Copy values" into them). There the lanterns' light comes with the torii, and the text, button and toolbar keep the app's own timing. The name stands still until everything the scene needs has been made (`dynSceneReady`: the landscape baked, the trees' clumps and first leaves, each part's drops in `rvPrecompute`; at most `NAME_WAIT_MAX`, 6 s, longer), so no heavy work is left for the reveal's frames. The reveal starts on the first frame after the name begins to fade (no pause): the sun and sky come in at once and every other part waits only until what it shows has been baked (`rvReady`, `InkReveal.release`), then starts at its own time or straight away; the first message comes at `RV_MESSAGE_AT` (4.0 s) on the reveal's clock (`rvBambooIn`). The fluid's graphics context is set up while the name stands still and its pictures are let go when the reveal ends; if the graphics chip can't be used it falls back to the simple wet paper (a safety stop after 40 s shows whatever is left). `inkreveal.js` copies the fluid's tiles once a frame into an ordinary canvas and cuts every part's mask from that copy (drawing a WebGL canvas straight into a 2D one makes the browser copy it each time). `window.RVDebug.log` has its timeline for the console.
- While the scene runs, nothing big is re-made for small changes of the light: the leaves and the trees' clumps follow every change (`lookVersion`, a few a frame), but the landscape and the bamboo are re-made only when the light has really changed (`bigLightV`: a new part of the day, or a change past `BIG_LIGHT_STEP`). The clouds are painted in the background by `clouds.js`'s worker (`Clouds.paintAsync`). The leaves a gust shakes off come one a frame (`gustDrops`).
- Smoothness rules already in place (keep them when changing the scene): nothing reads pixels back from a kept picture as a routine. The check for wiped pictures (`winBlankRow` in `landCheck` and `winCheck`) runs every 3 s only for 20 s after the page comes back or the graphics change state (`wipeWatch`), otherwise every 30 s (`wipeEvery`). The sun's pieces are made while the name stands still (`sunPrebake`) and, when its colour changes later, one piece a frame (`sunBakeLeft`). The lake's simulated waves: their shaders are compiled on the name screen and their first shape worked out while the name stands still (`Water.warm`, `lakeWarmWaves`); when the wind changes later `fftwaves.js` works the new waves out about 3 ms a frame (`rebuildSome`), the old ones showing meanwhile. In winter a change of the light re-makes the snowfield one swell a frame (`winBakeGroundStep`), then the snowdrift, then the sprites, each in a frame of its own, and the snow on the branches a few pieces a frame (`umeBakeSnowLater`, `umeSnowSome`); everything is still made at once while the scene is not showing yet. A resize event with the same size does nothing (`resize` in section 9), so the layers are not wiped.
- "Option N" (the user's naming): the services in `presets.json` are numbered by their place in the file. The first preset is Option 1, the second is Option 2, and so on. This means the preset's position (counting from 1), not its name, and not a row of the service table. If the user says "Option 3", read `presets.json` and take the third entry. If presets are added, removed or reordered, the numbers follow the new order. In the code, `buildNameScreenRows` tags each preset row with `data-option` (1, 2, 3 ...), the tag is copied to the matching row of the main service table when the name is confirmed, and `applyOptionRules` in `app.js` uses it for the user's greying-out rules for Options 1-5. The "その他" rows are not options.
- The text is in Japanese.
- It only runs on one Galaxy Tab S8 Ultra.
- Always explain changes in plain English.
- Other files may be added or changed when it helps, for example splitting code out of `app.js` into its own `.js` file.
- When adding a file the app uses, add it to the `ASSETS` list in `sw.js` and bump the `CACHE` version so the tablet picks it up.
- The small (far) maple's crown is kept balanced (the big, near maple is left as it was). In `buildKoyoTree` in `app.js` (and in `reveal-lab.html`): `balanceTrunk` moves the small tree in so its whole crown is on the screen with the trunk in the middle (the crown squeezed a little at most, to the width limit), and the trunk side may not reach farther than the outer side; `fillTrunkSide` then mirrors each row so the two sides match, with no cropping at the screen edge. Finally `evenSmallCrown` makes the small crown symmetric about the point where the trunk enters it (the trunk leans, so its foot is not the middle): the clumps on the outer side of that line are kept and mirrored onto the other side (their own colours). Then the tree is moved toward the screen edge so the crown stops just short of the pagoda (`LAKE.pagodaX`); its far side may run off the screen (the user's choice). The big maple keeps the original rule (its crown runs off the screen edge).
- Landmark rule for the autumn maples (the user's rule; never remove, bypass or weaken it): the big (near) maple always stands on the left and the small (far) one on the right (`buildKoyoTree` in `app.js`, no longer the daily `ENV.side`), and neither tree may ever hide Mt Fuji, the pagoda or the shrine hall completely (covering part of one is the worst allowed). `koyoKeepLandmarks` is the last step of `buildKoyoTree`: a short upright line down the middle of each landmark, from its top (`koyoLandmarks`; the pagoda's and hall's spots come from `lakeLandmarkSpots`, which the headland bake uses too), may be covered over at most three quarters of its length (`KOYO_LANDMARK_SHOW`); a tree that covers more is moved toward its own screen edge, then its crown is squeezed toward its trunk. Any later change to the trees, Fuji (`MTN3D.fujiX` / `fujiHeight`), the pagoda (`LAKE.pagodaX`) or the hall must keep this working; check it in the running app.
- Do exactly what the user asks, and change only the things they name. Never override or widen an instruction. If you have a suggestion, or the request is unclear (for example which tree is meant), ask first and wait for the answer before changing anything. Do not make changes that would catch the user off guard.
- Working rules (set by the user, follow them every session):
  1. Pin down the target first. If a request does not say which element is meant (for example which tree), ask which one before touching any code.
  2. Change only the element the user named. If a function is shared with other elements, limit the change to the named one; if that is not possible, ask first.
  3. Check before saying "fixed". Only say a fix works if it was actually run and looked at. If it could not be run, say so first and never describe it as done.
  4. Do not second-guess the user's judgment. Do not call something broken, or fine, against what the user says they see.
  5. Keep it small: one change per request, with no extras.
